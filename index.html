Here's Something Many People Don't Know

Studying abroad does not always mean paying the full cost yourself.

There are merit-based scholarship opportunities that can provide substantial funding, and in some cases fully fund eligible education and other approved expenses, depending on the scholarship's terms. Some universities and programmes are also considerably more affordable than many people assume.

The question is not simply "Can I afford to study abroad?" The better question is: "Do I know where to look, what I qualify for, what it will actually cost, and which options are realistic for me?"

A student with a modest budget may simply be looking at the wrong countries, universities, programmes or funding options — because they do not have the right information.

Many people think the only way to work abroad is a work visa.

That is not necessarily the only legitimate route. Depending on the country and its immigration rules, there are legitimate routes that can allow eligible people to enter a country for study and, where permitted, work under the conditions attached to their status — and potentially pursue lawful post-study opportunities.

A study route is not a work visa.

What matters is understanding the legal conditions attached to different immigration routes before spending money. Czechia, Lithuania and Bulgaria each have specific student work and post-study rules, which are covered in the toolkit.

The difference between you and the agent is often information.

Many people assume that studying abroad is only for the very wealthy. Part of the reason is that the process can look complicated and inaccessible when it is presented to you only through an intermediary. But the process itself is not magic.

An experienced agent may have more familiarity with the process, know where to look, understand the sequence of steps and know how to organize the information. That information is not rocket science — you should understand the fundamentals yourself before paying someone to help you navigate the process.

Before you pay an agent, know what you are paying for.

An agent can help you navigate an application, but there are important documents and information YOU must provide or prepare. An agent cannot manufacture your identity documents, academic history, financial evidence or personal experience.

Depending on the destination, institution and programme, you may need to prepare documents such as:

Personal & Identity Documents

  • International passport
  • Passport photographs where required
  • Birth certificate or other civil-status documentation where applicable

Academic Documents

  • WAEC/NECO or equivalent secondary-school credentials
  • Academic transcripts
  • Degree/NCE certificates where applicable
  • Other academic certificates required for the chosen programme

Application & Professional Documents

  • CV / Europass CV
  • Statement of Purpose (SOP)
  • Motivation Letter
  • Study plan or personal statement where required
  • Recommendation/reference letters where required
  • Evidence of previous work or professional experience where relevant

Financial Documents

  • Bank statements or other acceptable proof of funds
  • Sponsorship evidence where applicable
  • Scholarship/funding documentation where applicable
  • Other financial evidence required by the institution or immigration authority

Travel, Visa & Supporting Documents

  • Admission/offer documentation
  • Proof of accommodation where required
  • Health/insurance documentation where required
  • Visa/residence application forms
  • Required fee payment evidence
  • Authentication/legalisation/translation where applicable

Exact requirements vary by country, institution, programme and immigration route. Always verify current requirements with the relevant institution and government authority.
